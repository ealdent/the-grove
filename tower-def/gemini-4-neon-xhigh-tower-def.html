Master Jiro's Kiln Journal

Built into the pine-clad hillside of Seto-no-Mori, Kiln Seven is the last wood-fired climbing tunnel kiln still breathing in the valley. For four centuries it fired temple tiles, heirloom tea bowls, and mended saucers—absorbing the quiet patience of every potter who knelt before its ash-flue.

Tonight marks the Autumn Solstice Firing of the Heart Chawan. Master Jiro's hands have grown too frail to tend the seventy-two-hour burn alone, so he entrusted the chamber to his granddaughter Mina alongside five eccentric workshop contraptions cobbled from porcelain saucers, horsehair brushes, cinnabar lacquer pots, celadon bells, and kick-wheels.

As temperatures climb from 420°C to 1,320°C across 24 Watches, restless kiln flaws—crazing spiders, raw bisque lumps, soot moths, armored stoneware jugs, and towering slag colossi—surge upward from the firebox. Should any flaw strike the Heart Chawan, its fracture is immediately mended with 24k molten gold lacquer (Kintsugi), etching a permanent golden vein across the kiln slab and amplifying every instrument's power by +3% per mended seam.
