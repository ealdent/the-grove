How to use this guide

Each work is treated as a species. Its name is followed by the authority, the model that described it, and the year; then its habitat, a description, and the date it was first recorded here.

If you know what you want, go straight to the family. If you do not, use the key on the right: at each numbered couplet choose the line that fits, and follow the number it gives until you reach a family.

A tick beside a species marks it as seen. The checklist at the back keeps your list in this browser.

    About this direction

    A pocket field guide in the manner of the mid-century natural-history series: a frontispiece that changes with the date, a dichotomous key that walks a newcomer to the right family by asking what they want rather than what the folders are called, plates numbered and grouped by family, and species accounts where the model that made a work stands exactly where a taxonomic authority stands after a Latin name. The four benchmark briefs become four large genera, so "same brief, many models" is simply a family with many species. The checklist at the back is the one thing a visitor can leave behind: what they have seen.
