# Data centers, elections and public opinion

Research cutoff: November 4, 2026 (America/New_York). Findings for `learn/us-datacenter-grid-map.html`; election returns remain unofficial until certified.

## Election evidence

- **Ballotpedia's November 4 scorecard: 46 of 47 decided 2026 measures favored limits.** Its universe contains 50 tracked measures: 41 on November 3, six earlier, three later. Derived November 3 result: **40 of 41 favored limits**, because all six earlier votes favored limits. This is a count of heterogeneous local measures, including advisory questions, not 40 bans or a national referendum. [Ballotpedia tracker](https://ballotpedia.org/Data_center-related_ballot_measures,_2026) (updated November 4; original tracker, not an election authority).
- **Sunbury, Ohio:** 78.64% yes (2,809–763) on a charter restriction covering construction above 25 MW aggregate monthly demand or peak load. [Measure and unofficial result](https://ballotpedia.org/Sunbury,_Ohio,_Prohibit_Construction_of_Data_Centers_Initiative_(November_2026)) (100% precincts; November 4, 1:58 p.m. ET). [County results](https://boeresults.delco-gis.org/) explicitly says results are not certified; its dynamic contest figures were not accessible through the text browser.
- **Janesville, Wisconsin:** 62.82% yes (16,779–9,932) on requiring voter approval for GM/JATCO site development exceeding $450 million. This is a site-specific approval requirement, not a citywide data-center ban. [Measure and unofficial result](https://ballotpedia.org/Janesville,_Wisconsin,_Require_Voter_Approval_for_Development_of_GM/JATCO_Site_Exceeding_$450_Million_Initiative_(November_2026)) (100% precincts; November 4, 12:04 p.m. ET). [County source](https://www.co.rock.wi.us/departments/county-clerk/election-information/election-results-november-3-2026) returned HTTP 403 to the research browser.
- **Harwood, North Dakota:** 75.58% no (325–105), rejecting additional industrial-development review requirements, including for data centers. This was the tracker’s exception; it did not itself approve a specific facility. [Measure and unofficial result](https://ballotpedia.org/Harwood,_North_Dakota,_Industrial_Development_Review_Requirements_Initiative_(November_2026)) (100% precincts; November 4, 2:24 p.m. ET). [State results](https://resultsnd.sos.nd.gov/) require JavaScript for figures.
- **Additional geographic examples:** Alhambra, California, reported 82% for a ban; Bloomington, Illinois, 78% for an advisory moratorium; Wells, Maine, 82% for a 180-day moratorium. [Ballotpedia tracker](https://ballotpedia.org/Data_center-related_ballot_measures,_2026), November 4 table. Do not combine advisory votes with binding prohibitions without labeling them.

The tracker summary and the three individually opened result pages were read directly. Official links were followed, but their underlying numeric results were blocked or dynamically rendered. Use “reported results” with the unofficial label, not “certified.” Broader news tallies differ because of coverage and update timing; do not combine denominators.

## Public opinion and campaign context

- **Pew survey published September 22, 2026:** 60% of U.S. adults were not too/not at all comfortable with a new local data center; 54% of Republicans/Republican leaners and 68% of Democrats/Democratic leaners said so. Survey: July 20–August 9, 10,548 adults. Shares saying data centers are mostly bad for home energy costs rose from 38% in January to 50% in August; environment 39% → 54%; nearby quality of life 30% → 49%. These measure perceptions, not measured utility-bill or environmental impacts. [Pew original survey report and tables](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/22/americans-views-of-data-centers-have-turned-more-negative/).
- **Pew October 8 report:** 61% of registered voters disliked political leaders supporting nearby data centers; 8% liked them. Republican-candidate supporters: 44% dislike / 14% like; Democratic-candidate supporters: 75% / 5%. Survey: September 28–October 4, 5,062 adults including 3,917 registered voters. [Pew original report](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/10/08/with-americans-in-a-sour-mood-democratic-voters-are-more-engaged-in-midterms/).
- **Pew October 29 ad analysis:** among political Facebook/Instagram ads mentioning data centers, 57% were critical and 3% supportive during October through the 24th. Its full January–October sample was 20,751 ads. In July 27–October 24, data-center ads accounted for 13.9% of political ad spending targeted to Wisconsin, 7.0% in Ohio and 4.9% in Virginia. This is ad content/spending, not voter opinion or the proportion of votes caused by the issue. [Pew original ad analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/10/29/on-facebook-and-instagram-political-ads-are-increasingly-critical-of-data-centers/).

## Implementation interpretation

Election evidence supports showing a separate local approval/permission layer alongside power and construction constraints. It does not yield a defensible numerical reduction in national 2030 electricity demand on the day after voting. Label any user-controlled delay scenario as illustrative rather than a revised published forecast. Distinguish a local ban, advisory vote, moratorium, and additional approval requirement. Do not color an entire state as banned because one municipality voted against construction. Avoid claiming AI determined a candidate’s victory without causal evidence.

No direct original Quinnipiac September poll was located in the bounded search; the widely repeated 73%/21% figure is excluded. No new state-wide Virginia, Georgia or Michigan restriction is inferred from election reporting.
