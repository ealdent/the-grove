The fine print

Where the record is fuzzy

  • ChatGPT’s 100 million users in two months is an analyst’s estimate, not a figure OpenAI published.
  • The pause letter is dated 22 March 2023 but went public on the 28th. Its 30,000 signatures are the eventual total; it launched with about a thousand.
  • Geoffrey Hinton told Google he was leaving in April 2023. The first of May is the day he said why in public.
  • Nvidia’s $1 trillion on 30 May 2023 was a touch during the trading day, on the same day as the extinction statement. $2 trillion is the first close above it; $4 and $5 trillion are the days it first crossed.
  • “Back”, on Tuesday 21 November 2023, was a deal for Sam Altman’s return, announced late that night in California. He was formally reinstated on the 29th.
  • The Times lawsuit widened a copyright fight that authors had already started that summer.
  • “Washington bans Claude” is the 27 February 2026 order ending federal use, after Anthropic refused a Pentagon ultimatum. The courts have since split on it.
  • The “AGI era” was a hedged remark by OpenAI’s president at the GPT-6 launch, not a declaration by the company.
  • $3 trillion in one session is the S&P 500’s loss on 5 November 2026. Reports put it between $2.9 and $3.2 trillion.
  • $1.5 trillion is Anthropic’s fully diluted value at the $83 offer price. The shares did open the next day, 19 November, at $111.50.
  • Jensen Huang’s card says only that he resigned and that Nvidia slipped below $4 trillion. It gives no reason, on purpose.
  • Nobody real is pictured. Every image is an AI-generated illustration of an anonymous scene; the names, dates and numbers are type.
Liner notes

How a 1915 record came to narrate this

The voice

On 8 July 1915 the actor Taylor Holmes stood at a recording horn in New York and read Rudyard Kipling’s “Boots” for the Victor Talking Machine Company. Kipling wrote it in 1903 about infantry columns in the Boer War: men who march so long that the only thing left in their heads is the feet in front of them.

Holmes starts level and ends screaming. The recording is in the public domain. This copy is the Library of Congress transfer of Victor 55057, and the film starts 33 seconds in, where the poem does.

The cut

Speech has no tempo, so there is no beat grid. Every cut sits on a measured syllable of the recitation: 238 of them in 156 seconds. The boot stomps are built from the same list, so sound and picture cannot drift apart.

Eight stanzas, eight eras. Seven are black, white and red. The eighth, the one that says try to think of something different, turns to colour.

The point

The poem is about being unable to stop. So is the last nine years: a research paper, then a chatbot, then a hundred million people, a trillion dollars, a firing, a Nobel Prize, a ban, an escape.

The danger is real, and this is not a science fiction movie. So is the promise. Nobody gets to sit this one out, and nobody has to face it alone.
