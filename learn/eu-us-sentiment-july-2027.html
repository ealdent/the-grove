Public opinion · EU27

Broadly negative.
Still transactional.

Europe’s view of the United States has shifted from alliance by default to cooperation by necessity. The U.S. image is poor across most of the EU, confidence in current U.S. leadership is lower still, and support for greater European security autonomy is rising.

This site does not blend unlike questions into a made-up “sentiment score.” It lets you switch among image, direction, leadership, reliability, national interests and policy handling, with source coverage shown for every measure.

Sentiment anatomy

Negative does not mean disengaged

Across sources, Europeans distinguish between liking the U.S., trusting its current leadership, needing it as a partner and depending on it for defense.

Relationship model
Two different questions produce the same structural answer: affinity is weak, but strategic disengagement is not the majority position.

Country patterns

Four useful reads

01 · Hardest skepticism

Northern and western members set the negative ceiling

Denmark, the Netherlands, Germany, Sweden and Belgium all record negative U.S. images above 80%. Their “wrong direction” readings are even higher.

DK 85%NL 85%DE 84%SE 84%BE 82%
02 · Less negative east

Central and eastern Europe is not one pro-U.S. bloc

Romania is the clear positive outlier. Poland and Hungary are less negative than the EU average, while Slovakia and Slovenia sit near the skeptical western group.

RO 39%PL 50%HU 54%SK 77%SI 78%
03 · Fast deterioration

The sharpest change is not confined to western Europe

Negative opinion rose most in Slovakia, Greece, Malta, Lithuania and the Baltic region. The shift is broad, not just a France–Germany story.

SK +29GR +27MT +25LT +24EE/LV +22
04 · Pragmatic outliers

Hungary and Poland still see a usable partner

They are the only EU countries in Pew’s sample where majorities call the U.S. reliable. Yet majorities in both still disapprove of Trump’s tariff policy.

What the site is built from

The most recent comparable cross-country datasets available by 31 July 2026. Fieldwork dates matter more than publication dates.

26,415 face-to-face interviews across all 27 EU members. Provides the common country baseline: positive/negative image and whether the U.S. is going in the right or wrong direction.

Gallup World Poll

Gallup analysis ↗
Do not read small cross-source differences as movement. Eurobarometer and Pew use different wording, samples, field periods and interview modes. For example, “positive view,” “favorable opinion,” “reliable partner” and “ally” are separate constructs. The interface keeps them separate and marks countries not covered by a source. The Eurobarometer detailed annex reports 73% total negative EU27, while the Commission summary reports 74%; the hero therefore uses “≈74%.” Percentages may not sum to 100 because of rounding and nonresponse.
