Assumptions & math — a hypothetical cohort, separate from the published forecasts

Before disruption, the entire assumed pipeline opens at a constant rate from January 2027 through December 2030. Exposure is spread evenly across that schedule. Cancellations apply only to exposed capacity; every surviving exposed project receives the same added delay. Unexposed capacity keeps its original opening date.

Canceled = pipeline × exposure × cancellation rate
Deferred beyond 2030 = pipeline × exposure × (1 − cancellation rate) × delay months / 48
Online by end-2030 = pipeline − canceled − deferred

Percentages are fractions in these formulas; delay is limited to 0–48 months. Deferred projects eventually open by end-2034 in this model. No replacement projects, relocation, operating ramp, retirements or additional grid delays are modeled. Displayed values are rounded. This is new capacity in GW, not annual electricity in TWh; it does not revise the Berkeley Lab/EPRI forecasts or the mapped CBRE inventory.

04 / After November 3

A local vote on the buildout

In Ballotpedia's tracked November 3 measures, 40 of 41 outcomes favored limits. That includes advisory questions, approval requirements and bans—not 40 identical prohibitions.

Outcome favoring limitsHarwood exception

Derived from 46 limiting outcomes among 47 decided 2026 measures, minus six earlier votes. Reported November 4; unofficial. Harwood, ND rejected extra industrial review; it did not approve a specific project. Tracker and denominator ↗

The backlash extends across parties. In Pew's summer survey, 54% of Republicans and Republican leaners, and 68% of Democrats and Democratic leaners, were uncomfortable with a new nearby data center.

US adults · n=10,548 · July 20–August 9, 2026. Opinion measures public acceptance, not a measured effect on utility bills. Pew ↗

What this changes in the outlook

Local restrictions can change project locations and delivery dates. Existing facilities, projects with approvals and future proposals face different decisions. These published forecasts predate the vote; the results alone do not establish a new national TWh estimate.

How to read this update

Three measurements, three meanings

TWh/year is energy consumed over a year. MW or GW of market inventory is data-center capacity tracked by CBRE. Grid peak demand is power needed at a particular time. None is interchangeable with available power at a proposed site.

The map includes eight comparable CBRE markets, three grid observations and six illustrative local votes. It is not a census of every facility or every restriction. Marker positions are approximate market or jurisdiction centers.

What the election can tell us

Local votes provide evidence about siting and public acceptance. They do not show that AI alone caused candidate victories, or that every proposal in a state is prohibited. Reported returns may change before certification.

Grid adequacy depends on local delivery and timing; national nameplate capacity does not measure power available at a site. State geometry is from the US Census Bureau's 2024 cartographic boundaries, projected using Albers equal-area.
